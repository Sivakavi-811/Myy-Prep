# 05 — Simple and Compound Interest

## Key Formulas & Shortcuts

| Concept | Formula |
|---|---|
| Simple Interest | **SI = P × R × T / 100** |
| Amount (SI) | **A = P + SI** |
| Compound Interest amount | **A = P × (1 + R/100)ᵀ** |
| Compound Interest | **CI = A − P** |
| CI − SI for 2 years | **P × (R/100)²** |
| CI − SI for 3 years | **P × (R/100)² × (3 + R/100)** |

> **Shortcut:** Under SI, money doubles in **100 / R** years. Under CI, the **Rule of 72** says money doubles in about **72 / R** years.

---

## Question 1

Find the simple interest on **₹5,000** at **8% per annum** for **3 years**.

### Solution

1. SI = 5000 × 8 × 3 / 100
2. = **₹1,200**

### ✅ Answer: **₹1,200**

---

## Question 2

Find the compound interest on **₹10,000** at **10% per annum** for **2 years**, compounded annually.

### Solution

1. A = 10000 × (1.1)² = 10000 × 1.21 = **₹12,100**
2. CI = 12100 − 10000 = **₹2,100**

**Year-by-year check:** Year 1 earns 1000. Year 2 earns 1000 + 10% of 1000 = 1100. Total = 2100 ✔

### ✅ Answer: **₹2,100**

---

## Question 3

The difference between CI and SI on a sum at **5% per annum** for **2 years** is **₹25**. Find the sum.

### Solution

1. CI − SI (2 years) = P × (R/100)²
2. 25 = P × (5/100)² = P × 1/400
3. P = 25 × 400 = **₹10,000**

### ✅ Answer: **₹10,000**

---

## Takeaways

- SI earns the same interest every year. CI also earns **interest on interest**.
- For a 2-year CI − SI question, use **P(R/100)²** directly. Don't calculate CI and SI separately.
- For CI, multiply by the growth factor (1 + R/100) once for each year.
