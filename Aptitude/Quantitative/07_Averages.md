# 07 — Averages

## Key Formulas & Shortcuts

| Concept | Formula |
|---|---|
| Average | **Sum of observations / Number of observations** |
| Sum from average | **Sum = Average × Count** |
| Average of the first n natural numbers | **(n + 1) / 2** |
| Average of consecutive numbers (or any arithmetic series) | **(First + Last) / 2** |
| New member joins and the average changes by d | New value = **Old average + (new count × d)** |

> **Deviation trick:** Pick a convenient base value. Average = base + (sum of deviations from the base) / count. This avoids adding up large numbers.

---

## Question 1

Find the average of **42, 45, 48, 51, 54**.

### Solution (deviation method)

1. Take a base of 48. The deviations are −6, −3, 0, +3, +6.
2. The sum of deviations is **0**.
3. Average = 48 + 0/5 = **48**

**Alternative:** the numbers form an arithmetic series, so average = (42 + 54)/2 = 48 ✔

### ✅ Answer: **48**

---

## Question 2

The average age of **30 students** is **15 years**. When the teacher's age is included, the average rises by **1 year**. Find the teacher's age.

### Solution

1. The new average is 16 over 31 people.
2. Teacher's age = new average + (old count × increase) = 16 + 30 × 1 = **46**

**Check:** new sum = 31 × 16 = 496. Old sum = 30 × 15 = 450. Teacher = 496 − 450 = 46 ✔

### ✅ Answer: **46 years**

---

## Question 3

A batsman's average after **16 innings** rises by **3** when he scores **87** in the 17th innings. Find his new average.

### Solution

1. Let the old average be x. Then 16x + 87 = 17(x + 3)
2. 16x + 87 = 17x + 51, so x = **36**
3. New average = 36 + 3 = **39**

### ✅ Answer: **39**

---

## Takeaways

- Change the average into a **total** (average × count) and the problem becomes simple arithmetic.
- When someone joins and the average rises by d, they contributed **d for every member**, including themselves, on top of the old average.
- The deviation method makes averages of large numbers quick to work out mentally.
