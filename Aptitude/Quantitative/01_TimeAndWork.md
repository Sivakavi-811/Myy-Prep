# 01 — Time and Work

## Key Formulas & Shortcuts

| Concept | Formula |
|---|---|
| One day's work | If A finishes a job in **n** days, A does **1/n** of it per day |
| Combined work | A and B together take **(a × b) / (a + b)** days |
| Total-work (LCM) method | Take total work = LCM of the individual days; then each person's rate = total ÷ their days |
| Efficiency | Efficiency is inversely proportional to time taken |

> **LCM trick:** Set total work = LCM of the given days so every rate is a whole number. This avoids fractions and is usually the fastest method in exams.

---

## Question 1

A can complete a piece of work in **12 days** and B can complete it in **18 days**. In how many days will they finish it working together?

### Solution (LCM method)

1. Total work = LCM(12, 18) = **36 units**
2. A's rate = 36 ÷ 12 = **3 units/day**
3. B's rate = 36 ÷ 18 = **2 units/day**
4. Combined rate = 3 + 2 = **5 units/day**
5. Time = 36 ÷ 5 = **7.2 days**

**Check with the shortcut:** (12 × 18) / (12 + 18) = 216 / 30 = 7.2 ✔

### ✅ Answer: **7.2 days** (7 1/5 days)

---

## Question 2

A and B together can do a job in **10 days**. A alone can do it in **15 days**. A and B start together, but A leaves after **4 days**. How many more days will B take to finish the remaining work?

### Solution

1. Total work = LCM(10, 15) = **30 units**
2. (A + B)'s rate = 30 ÷ 10 = **3 units/day**
3. A's rate = 30 ÷ 15 = **2 units/day** → B's rate = 3 − 2 = **1 unit/day**
4. Work done in the first 4 days = 4 × 3 = **12 units**
5. Remaining work = 30 − 12 = **18 units**
6. B alone finishes it in 18 ÷ 1 = **18 days**

### ✅ Answer: **18 more days**

---

## Takeaways

- Convert everything to **units of work** with the LCM. Rates then add and subtract cleanly.
- If a worker joins or leaves partway, split the timeline into phases and total the work done in each phase.
