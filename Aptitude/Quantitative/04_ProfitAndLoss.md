# 04 — Profit and Loss

## Key Formulas & Shortcuts

| Concept | Formula |
|---|---|
| Profit / Loss | **Profit = SP − CP**, **Loss = CP − SP** |
| Profit % | **Profit / CP × 100** (always measured on cost price) |
| SP from profit % | **SP = CP × (100 + P%) / 100** |
| SP from loss % | **SP = CP × (100 − L%) / 100** |
| Marked price & discount | **SP = MP × (100 − D%) / 100** |
| Same SP, x% gain on one item and x% loss on the other | Always a net **loss of x²/100 %** |

> **Multiplier trick:** A 20% profit means SP = 1.2 × CP. A 25% loss means SP = 0.75 × CP. Thinking in multipliers turns most questions into a single multiplication or division.

---

## Question 1

A shopkeeper buys an article for **₹800** and sells it for **₹1,000**. Find the profit percentage.

### Solution

1. Profit = 1000 − 800 = **₹200**
2. Profit % = 200 / 800 × 100 = **25%**

### ✅ Answer: **25% profit**

---

## Question 2

A trader marks an item **40% above** its cost price and then gives a **20% discount**. What is the profit percentage?

### Solution (multiplier method)

1. Let CP = 100
2. MP = 100 × 1.4 = **140**
3. SP = 140 × 0.8 = **112**
4. Profit % = (112 − 100) / 100 × 100 = **12%**

**Shortcut:** net = +40 − 20 + (40 × −20)/100 = 20 − 8 = **12%** ✔

### ✅ Answer: **12% profit**

---

## Question 3

Two phones are each sold for **₹9,900**. One is sold at a **10% gain** and the other at a **10% loss**. What is the overall gain or loss percentage?

### Solution

1. Same SP with equal gain and loss of x% always gives a loss of **x²/100 %**
2. Loss % = 10² / 100 = **1%**

**Verify:**
- CP₁ = 9900 / 1.1 = 9000, CP₂ = 9900 / 0.9 = 11000, so total CP = 20000
- Total SP = 19800, so the loss is 200, and 200 / 20000 = 1% ✔

### ✅ Answer: **1% loss**

---

## Takeaways

- Profit and loss percentages are always calculated on the **cost price**. Discounts are calculated on the **marked price**.
- Set CP = 100 to turn percentages into simple numbers.
- Selling two items at the same price with an equal % gain and loss **always** gives a loss.
