# 08 — Mixtures and Alligation

## Key Formulas & Shortcuts

**Rule of Alligation.** Mixing a cheaper item (price c) with a dearer item (price d) to get a mean price m:

```
   Cheaper (c)        Dearer (d)
            \          /
             Mean (m)
            /          \
      (d − m)          (m − c)

Cheaper : Dearer = (d − m) : (m − c)
```

| Concept | Formula |
|---|---|
| Mixing ratio | **Cheaper : Dearer = (d − m) : (m − c)** |
| Repeated replacement (x litres taken out of V and refilled with water, n times) | Pure liquid left = **V × (1 − x/V)ⁿ** |

> **Tip:** Alligation works for any "average of two groups": prices, concentrations, speeds over equal times, or the average marks of two classes.

---

## Question 1

In what ratio must rice costing **₹30/kg** be mixed with rice costing **₹45/kg** so the mixture costs **₹35/kg**?

### Solution

1. c = 30, d = 45, m = 35
2. Cheaper : Dearer = (45 − 35) : (35 − 30) = **10 : 5 = 2 : 1**

### ✅ Answer: **2 : 1**

---

## Question 2

A **40-litre** mixture of milk and water contains **10% water**. How much water must be added to make water **20%** of the new mixture?

### Solution

1. Milk = 90% of 40 = **36 litres**, which stays the same.
2. In the new mixture milk is 80%, so new total = 36 / 0.8 = **45 litres**
3. Water added = 45 − 40 = **5 litres**

### ✅ Answer: **5 litres**

---

## Question 3

A container holds **80 litres** of milk. **8 litres** are taken out and replaced with water. This is done **2 times** in total. How much milk is left?

### Solution

1. Milk left = 80 × (1 − 8/80)²
2. = 80 × (0.9)² = 80 × 0.81 = **64.8 litres**

### ✅ Answer: **64.8 litres**

---

## Takeaways

- Alligation gives you the **ratio** directly: write the cross differences and simplify.
- When something is added, find the part that **doesn't change** (here, the milk) and build the answer around it.
- For repeated replacement, multiply by (1 − x/V) once for each round.
