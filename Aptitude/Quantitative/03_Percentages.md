# 03 — Percentages

## Key Formulas & Shortcuts

| Concept | Formula |
|---|---|
| Percentage change | **(New − Old) / Old × 100** |
| Successive changes of a% and b% | Net change = **a + b + (a × b) / 100** (use − for a decrease) |
| A is x% more than B → B is less than A by | **x / (100 + x) × 100 %** |
| Price up r% → cut consumption by this to keep spend the same | **r / (100 + r) × 100 %** |

> **Fraction trick:** Learn the common fractions: 12.5% = 1/8, 16.67% = 1/6, 20% = 1/5, 25% = 1/4, 33.33% = 1/3. Most exam numbers are built from them.

---

## Question 1

The price of a shirt is first increased by **20%** and then decreased by **20%**. What is the net percentage change in price?

### Solution (successive change shortcut)

1. a = +20, b = −20
2. Net change = 20 + (−20) + (20 × −20) / 100
3. = 0 − 4 = **−4%**

**Check with numbers:** 100 → 120 → 120 × 0.8 = 96, so the price falls by 4 ✔

### ✅ Answer: **4% decrease**

---

## Question 2

A's salary is **25% more** than B's. By what percentage is B's salary less than A's?

### Solution

1. Use the shortcut x / (100 + x) × 100 with x = 25
2. = 25 / 125 × 100
3. = **20%**

**Check with numbers:** B = 100, A = 125. The difference is 25, and 25 / 125 = 20% ✔

### ✅ Answer: **20% less**

---

## Question 3

The price of sugar rises by **25%**. By what percentage must a family cut its consumption so its spending on sugar stays the same?

### Solution

1. Reduction = r / (100 + r) × 100 with r = 25
2. = 25 / 125 × 100 = **20%**

### ✅ Answer: **20%**

---

## Takeaways

- Changes are always measured against the **original (base)** value. Most traps come from using the wrong base.
- For successive changes, never just add the percentages. Use **a + b + ab/100**.
- Plug in 100 as a starting value to check any answer quickly.
